Isotope Geochemistry¶

Oxygen isotope notations¶

Oxygen has three stable isotopes:

  • 16O^{16}\mathrm{O}16O

  • 17O^{17}\mathrm{O}17O

  • 18O^{18}\mathrm{O}18O

In stable isotope geochemistry, we usually compare the ratio of a heavy isotope to the most abundant isotope, 16O^{16}\mathrm{O}16O.

For oxygen isotopes:

R17=17O16OR^{17} = \frac{^{17}\mathrm{O}}{^{16}\mathrm{O}}R17=16O17O​
(1)
R18=18O16OR^{18} = \frac{^{18}\mathrm{O}}{^{16}\mathrm{O}}R18=16O18O​
(2)

Because absolute isotope ratios are very small and difficult to compare directly, isotope compositions are commonly reported relative to a reference material, such as VSMOW.

The delta notation is defined as:

δxO=(RsamplexRreferencex−1)×1000\delta^{x}\mathrm{O} = \left( \frac{R^{x}_{\mathrm{sample}}}{R^{x}_{\mathrm{reference}}} -1 \right) \times 1000δxO=(Rreferencex​Rsamplex​​−1)×1000
(3)

where xxx can be 17 or 18.

For example:

δ18O=((18O/16O)sample(18O/16O)reference−1)×1000\delta^{18}\mathrm{O} = \left( \frac{ \left(^{18}\mathrm{O}/^{16}\mathrm{O}\right)_{\mathrm{sample}} }{ \left(^{18}\mathrm{O}/^{16}\mathrm{O}\right)_{\mathrm{reference}} } -1 \right) \times 1000δ18O=((18O/16O)reference​(18O/16O)sample​​−1)×1000
(4)

The result is reported in per mil, written as ‰.

A positive δ\deltaδ value means that the sample has a higher heavy-isotope/light-isotope ratio than the reference.

A negative δ\deltaδ value means that the sample has a lower heavy-isotope/light-isotope ratio than the reference.

Linearized delta notation: δ′\delta'δ′ notation¶

In triple oxygen isotope geochemistry, we often use the linearized form of delta notation.

This is called delta-prime notation:

δ′xO=1000ln⁡(1+δxO1000)\delta'^{x}\mathrm{O} = 1000 \ln \left( 1+ \frac{\delta^{x}\mathrm{O}}{1000} \right)δ′xO=1000ln(1+1000δxO​)
(5)

where xxx can be 17 or 18.

Therefore:

δ′17O=1000ln⁡(1+δ17O1000)\delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} = 1000 \ln \left( 1+ \frac{\delta^{17}\mathrm{O}}{1000} \right)δ′17O=1000ln(1+1000δ17O​)
(6)

and

δ′18O=1000ln⁡(1+δ18O1000)\delta'^{18}\mathrm{O} = 1000 \ln \left( 1+ \frac{\delta^{18}\mathrm{O}}{1000} \right)δ′18O=1000ln(1+1000δ18O​)
(7)

For small isotope variations, δ\deltaδ and δ′\delta'δ′ are very similar.

However, δ′\delta'δ′ is mathematically useful because isotope fractionation becomes additive in logarithmic space.

Fractionation factor¶

The isotope fractionation factor between two phases, A and B, is defined as:

αA−Bx=RAxRBx\alpha^{x}_{A-B} = \frac{R^{x}_{A}}{R^{x}_{B}}αA−Bx​=RBx​RAx​​
(8)

where RxR^{x}Rx is the isotope ratio of isotope xxx relative to 16O^{16}\mathrm{O}16O.

For oxygen isotopes:

αA−B18=(18O/16O)A(18O/16O)B\alpha^{18}_{A-B} = \frac{ \left(^{18}\mathrm{O}/^{16}\mathrm{O}\right)_A }{ \left(^{18}\mathrm{O}/^{16}\mathrm{O}\right)_B }αA−B18​=(18O/16O)B​(18O/16O)A​​
(9)

The fractionation factor can also be calculated from delta values:

αA−Bx=1+δxOA/10001+δxOB/1000\alpha^{x}_{A-B} = \frac{ 1+\delta^{x}O_A/1000 }{ 1+\delta^{x}O_B/1000 }αA−Bx​=1+δxOB​/10001+δxOA​/1000​
(10)

In isotope geochemistry, fractionation is often reported as:

1000ln⁡αA−Bx1000 \ln \alpha^{x}_{A-B}1000lnαA−Bx​
(11)

Using delta-prime notation:

1000ln⁡αA−Bx=δ′xOA−δ′xOB1000 \ln \alpha^{x}_{A-B} = \delta'^{x}O_A - \delta'^{x}O_B1000lnαA−Bx​=δ′xOA​−δ′xOB​
(12)

This is one reason why the logarithmic notation is useful.

Triple oxygen isotope relationship¶

In mass-dependent fractionation, 17O^{17}\mathrm{O}17O and 18O^{18}\mathrm{O}18O usually vary together.

Because 17O^{17}\mathrm{O}17O is intermediate in mass between 16O^{16}\mathrm{O}16O and 18O^{18}\mathrm{O}18O, the change in δ17O\delta^{17}\mathrm{O}δ17O is approximately about half of the change in δ18O\delta^{18}\mathrm{O}δ18O.

In linearized notation, this relationship is commonly written as:

δ′17O=λδ′18O+γ\delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} = \lambda \delta'^{18}\mathrm{O} + \gammaδ′17O=λδ′18O+γ
(13)

where:

  • λ\lambdaλ is the slope of the reference line

  • γ\gammaγ is the intercept

  • δ′17O\delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}δ′17O and δ′18O\delta'^{18}\mathrm{O}δ′18O are the linearized isotope values

A commonly used simplified form is:

δ′17O=λδ′18O\delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} = \lambda \delta'^{18}\mathrm{O}δ′17O=λδ′18O
(14)

with, for example:

λ=0.528\lambda = 0.528λ=0.528
(15)

This line represents the expected mass-dependent relationship between 17O^{17}\mathrm{O}17O and 18O^{18}\mathrm{O}18O.

Capital delta (Cap Delta) notation: Δ′17O\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}Δ′17O¶

The parameter Δ′17O\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}Δ′17O describes the deviation of a sample from a chosen mass-dependent reference line.

It is commonly defined as:

Δ′17O=δ′17O−λδ′18O\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} = \delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} - \lambda \delta'^{18}\mathrm{O}Δ′17O=δ′17O−λδ′18O
(16)

where λ\lambdaλ is the chosen reference slope.

If the reference line includes an intercept, the equation becomes:

Δ′17O=δ′17O−(λδ′18O+γ)\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} = \delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} - \left( \lambda \delta'^{18}\mathrm{O} + \gamma \right)Δ′17O=δ′17O−(λδ′18O+γ)
(17)

If Δ′17O=0\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} = 0Δ′17O=0, the sample lies exactly on the chosen reference line.

If Δ′17O>0\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} > 0Δ′17O>0, the sample lies above the reference line.

If Δ′17O<0\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} < 0Δ′17O<0, the sample lies below the reference line.

Because Δ′17O\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}Δ′17O values are usually very small, they are often reported in per meg:

Δ′17Oper meg=1000[δ′17O−λδ′18O]\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{per\ meg}} = 1000 \left[ \delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} - \lambda \delta'^{18}\mathrm{O} \right]Δ′17Oper meg​=1000[δ′17O−λδ′18O]
(18)

Here, δ′17O\delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}δ′17O and δ′18O\delta'^{18}\mathrm{O}δ′18O are in per mil, while Δ′17Oper meg\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{per\ meg}}Δ′17Oper meg​ is in per meg.

θ\thetaθ and λ\lambdaλ¶

In triple oxygen isotope geochemistry, both θ\thetaθ and λ\lambdaλ describe slopes in oxygen isotope space, but they are often used differently.

The exponent θ\thetaθ is commonly used for a specific fractionation process between two phases:

θA−B=ln⁡αA−B17ln⁡αA−B18\theta_{A-B} = \frac{ \ln \alpha^{17}_{A-B} }{ \ln \alpha^{18}_{A-B} }θA−B​=lnαA−B18​lnαA−B17​​
(19)

For example, θ\thetaθ may describe equilibrium fractionation between quartz and water, or another specific mineral-fluid pair.

The parameter λ\lambdaλ is commonly used for a reference line or empirical relationship in isotope space:

δ′17O=λδ′18O+γ\delta'^{17}\mathrm{O} = \lambda \delta'^{18}\mathrm{O} + \gammaδ′17O=λδ′18O+γ
(20)

In simple terms:

  • θ\thetaθ describes the slope of a specific fractionation process.

  • λ\lambdaλ describes the slope of a chosen reference line.

Because Δ′17O\Delta'^{17}\mathrm{O}Δ′17O depends on the chosen reference slope, the value of λ\lambdaλ should always be reported.

Hyperbolic Mixing Equation¶

The isotopic ratio of the mixture is given by:

(87Sr86Sr)mix=[Sr]A⋅(87Sr86Sr)A⋅(1−x)+[Sr]B⋅(87Sr86Sr)B⋅x⋅r[Sr]A⋅(1−x)+[Sr]B⋅x⋅r\left( \frac{^{87}\text{Sr}}{^{86}\text{Sr}} \right)_{\text{mix}} = \frac{[\text{Sr}]_A \cdot \left( \frac{^{87}\text{Sr}}{^{86}\text{Sr}} \right)_A \cdot (1 - x) + [\text{Sr}]_B \cdot \left( \frac{^{87}\text{Sr}}{^{86}\text{Sr}} \right)_B \cdot x \cdot r}{[\text{Sr}]_A \cdot (1 - x) + [\text{Sr}]_B \cdot x \cdot r}(86Sr87Sr​)mix​=[Sr]A​⋅(1−x)+[Sr]B​⋅x⋅r[Sr]A​⋅(86Sr87Sr​)A​⋅(1−x)+[Sr]B​⋅(86Sr87Sr​)B​⋅x⋅r​
(21)

Where:

  • [Sr]A[\text{Sr}]_A[Sr]A​, [Sr]B[\text{Sr}]_B[Sr]B​ = Sr concentrations in end-members A (mantle) and B (crust)

  • r=[Sr]B[Sr]Ar = \frac{[\text{Sr}]_B}{[\text{Sr}]_A}r=[Sr]A​[Sr]B​​ = concentration ratio (5:1, 1:10, etc.)

  • xxx = mixing fraction (0 to 1)

δ18Omix=δ18OA(1−x)+δ18OB⋅x  (linear mixing for oxygen)\delta^{18}\text{O}_{\text{mix}} = \delta^{18}\text{O}_A (1 - x) + \delta^{18}\text{O}_B \cdot x \ \ \text{(linear mixing for oxygen)}δ18Omix​=δ18OA​(1−x)+δ18OB​⋅x  (linear mixing for oxygen)
